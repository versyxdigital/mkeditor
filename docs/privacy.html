MKEditor

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 23 November 2026

MKEditor is an open-source Markdown editor published by versyx Digital. This policy explains how the desktop application and browser editor handle your information. For privacy questions, contact privacy@versyx.digital.

You can use MKEditor to edit local documents without connecting Google Drive or an AI provider. Local editing does not upload your documents to a versyx Digital document-storage service. Optional connections send information to the services described below.

  • Documents and local storage
  • Google Drive
  • Optional AI assistance
  • Other network connections
  • Retention and deletion
  • Your choices and contact

Documents and local storage

MKEditor reads the files and folders you open and writes documents where you choose to save them. Editor contents, filenames, folder paths, preferences and session information are processed on your device to provide editing, preview, export and session restoration.

The desktop application stores settings and session information in ~/.mkeditor/ within your user home directory. Session data can include open-file paths, view state and untitled document contents. If you use the AI Assistant, conversations and related settings are also stored locally. Local diagnostic logs can contain file paths, error details and other information about application operations.

The browser editor uses your browser's local storage and IndexedDB for settings, saved sessions and folder-access handles. File access depends on the permissions you grant through the browser. Google Drive and AI provider connections described here are desktop features.

Saved API keys and account tokens use operating-system-backed encryption. Documents, conversation history, ordinary settings and session files are not encrypted by MKEditor itself; protection also depends on your device, browser, account permissions and backups.

Google Drive

Connecting Google Drive is optional. Sign-in takes place in your system browser with Google. MKEditor receives authorization tokens rather than your Google password. It obtains your account identifier and available email address or display name to label the connection and keep files bound to the correct account.

To display the folders you browse, MKEditor reads file and folder metadata such as identifiers, names, types, folder relationships, sizes, modification times, revision information and available permissions. Listings can contain metadata for file types other than Markdown, even though those entries cannot be opened in the editor. When you open a supported Markdown file, MKEditor downloads its contents for editing and preview.

Connections initially request read-only Drive access. If you separately choose Enable editing, Google requests broader Drive permission, whose consent description includes viewing, editing, creating and deleting Drive files. MKEditor uses this permission to browse folders, save chosen Markdown documents and create Markdown files or copies in your chosen My Drive folder. The application does not expose remote delete, rename or move commands. Shared-drive files remain read-only.

MKEditor sends file contents to Google when you save or create a Drive document. It also reads contents and revision metadata to detect conflicts or check an uncertain save result. New files include an application operation marker used to check creation without creating a duplicate. These requests travel directly between your desktop application and Google; they are not routed through a versyx Digital document server.

Connection names, account labels and encrypted tokens are stored locally in ~/.mkeditor/connections.json. Open document contents and unresolved save snapshots are held in application memory. Remote tabs are not restored after restart. Copies you save locally, text you share in AI conversations and operating-system backups can persist separately.

MKEditor's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements. Google user data is used to provide the visible features described in this policy. We do not sell it, use it for advertising or use it to train generalized AI models. Any sharing with an AI service occurs through the optional, user-controlled actions described below.

Optional AI assistance

If you use the AI Assistant, your messages, relevant conversation history and included document context are sent directly to the provider you choose: OpenAI, Anthropic or your configured Ollama endpoint. Tool requests and results may include file contents, filenames, paths and directory information from your workspace. Sending a request is not limited to the words typed into the chat box.

New conversations enable active local-file context by default; selection sharing is initially off. Review the conversation's context controls before sending, and disable active-file sharing if you do not want that file included. Files you mention and workspace information obtained through assistant tools can also become part of the provider request. Write tools ask for confirmation unless you enable automatic acceptance for that conversation.

Opening a Drive document alone does not send its full contents to an AI provider. Drive-derived text can be sent if you paste it into chat, enable sharing of selected text, or include a local copy through the assistant's context or tools. Use these controls only for information you intend to share with that provider.

API keys authenticate requests to the corresponding provider. If you choose ChatGPT plan sign-in, MKEditor also stores account identifiers, an email label, authorization state and encrypted account tokens locally. A locally generated installation identifier is used for that sign-in registration. Signing in and making plan requests communicate with OpenAI.

A local Ollama endpoint processes requests on your device; configuring a remote endpoint sends the same request information to that endpoint's operator. Providers' handling of information, including retention and available data controls, depends on the service and account you use. See the applicable OpenAI and Anthropic privacy information and your service agreement. MKEditor cannot remove information already retained by a provider simply by deleting a local conversation.

Other network connections and support

Desktop update checks and downloads contact the release-hosting infrastructure, including GitHub. Website and browser-editor visits contact the hosting service. Those services receive normal connection information such as your IP address and request details. The public MKEditor website is hosted using GitHub Pages; see GitHub's privacy statement.

Documents and exported HTML can contain remote images, links, fonts, scripts or other external resources. Displaying or following them may contact their hosts and disclose network and browser information. Some styled exports load presentation resources from content-delivery networks. This is separate from uploading your source document to an AI or Drive service.

MKEditor's application code does not include an analytics or automatic crash-report upload service. Local diagnostic logs are not automatically submitted to versyx Digital. If you contact us by email or submit an issue, we receive the information you choose to provide, such as your contact details, message, attachments or diagnostic logs. GitHub issues are public; do not include credentials or private documents in them.

Information you send us for support is used to respond, investigate reported problems and maintain the application. We do not sell your personal information or use your documents for advertising. Third-party services may process information in countries different from yours, under their applicable policies and agreements.

Retention and deletion

  • Local documents: remain wherever you save them until you delete them. Removing MKEditor or a connection does not delete documents or their backups.
  • Sessions and browser data: use Settings to clear the saved session. You can also clear the editor's site data through your browser. Clearing stored sessions does not delete source files.
  • Google Drive: Disconnect removes locally stored credentials and the account label; Remove also removes the connection configuration. Existing open buffers and local copies remain. Close buffers you no longer need. A storage failure is reported and may leave encrypted credentials on disk. You can separately revoke MKEditor's authorization through your Google account connections. Disconnecting does not delete files stored in Drive or undo a request already sent.
  • AI data: delete conversations in the Assistant, remove API keys in provider settings, or sign out of ChatGPT. Removing credentials does not delete conversation history. Third-party copies must be managed with the provider.
  • Application data: after closing MKEditor, you can remove its local data from ~/.mkeditor/. Back up anything you want to keep first; session data can contain untitled work and conversation history can contain document text. Operating-system and browser backups may retain additional copies.
  • Support correspondence: we retain information needed to respond to and resolve your inquiry, and where required for legal obligations. Contact us about deletion of information you have sent us; public issue records and third-party systems may require separate action.

Your choices and contact

You can continue editing local documents without Drive or AI connections. You control which accounts to connect, the assistant context you share and where you save documents. Google processes information under its privacy policy; revoking a connection stops future authorized access but does not remove data already stored elsewhere.

For privacy questions or applicable requests to access, correct or delete personal information held by versyx Digital, email privacy@versyx.digital. Most editor data is held on your device, so we cannot remotely inspect or erase those local files for you. Depending on your location, you may also have the right to complain to your data-protection authority.

We will update this page when these practices change and revise the date above. Changes to Google data access or use that require renewed consent will be explained before that new access or use occurs.
